The game in one breath

In [name], you run a tiny pottery studio: shape each customer's wish on a spinning wheel with your thumb, fire it, and see their face light up, until the last customer of the day walks in.

One run is one day in the studio: about 6–8 customers, from morning light to evening glow, about 8–10 minutes. That's short enough for a judge to finish, and long enough to feel like a small story.

One customer, start to finish

🚪 Customer arrives→ 💬 Wish + shape outline→ 🏺 Shape on the wheel→ 🎨 Tap a glaze→ 🔥 Kiln→ ✨ Reveal + reaction + stars→ 🪴 A copy goes on your shelf

If the clay collapses on the wheel: a floppy "sploosh", the customer giggles, and you get a fresh lump. No penalty except time.

The wheel: how the thumb shapes clay

Controls

Up / down Where your thumb is vertically = which part of the pot you touch (the rim, the belly, the base).

Left / right Your thumb's distance from the centre = how wide the clay gets at that height. Move towards the centre to squeeze it in, away to let it swell out.

Lift Take your thumb off and tap Done when you're happy.

The clay moves towards your thumb gently, not instantly, so it feels soft and wet, not like a slider.

How it's scored, and how it collapses

Match %: how close your shape is to the wish outline, measured at ~20 heights. 60% = ★, 80% = ★★, 92% = ★★★.

Glaze bonus: pick the colour the customer hinted at → a sparkle and a happier reaction.

Wobble rises when you squeeze a wall very thin, or build a heavy top on a thin neck. Full wobble = sploosh. It's a joke, not a punishment.

How it's built (simple version)

The pot is a list of about 24 widths, from bottom to top. Every frame, Godot spins that list into a round 3D shape (a "lathe", like a real wheel). Your thumb changes a few numbers in the list. That's the whole trick: one list of numbers is the pot, the score, the collapse check and the shelf copy.

Why keep playing: all three

Story

One day, 7 customers

Morning light → evening glow through the window. Each customer has one line of wish and one line of reaction. The last customer is special: the theme's twist lives there, and it's the ending.

Shelves

Your pots stay

Each finished pot gets a copy on the shelves, in your shape and your glaze. At the end, your whole day is on display. It's free to build (it reuses the pot you just made), and it's the screenshot people share.

Stars

1–3 stars per order

The total goes on the end screen (★ 17 / 21) with your best day. It gives a reason to replay the day, without ever failing you.

The look

Toon low-poly 3D, warm and soft. Light falls in 2–3 hard bands, with thick dark outlines, sunlight through one window, and nothing too saturated. The clay looks wet while shaping (a moving highlight) and turns glossy after the kiln.

Camera: fixed, slightly above the wheel, looking down a little, so you see both the shape and the top of the pot. Portrait makes the pot the hero of the screen.

Neutral for now. At 11 pm the theme may repaint the room, the customers and the glaze colours.

Three questions every time: who are the customers, what do they wish for, and who is the special last customer?

Codex workflow: one style, no drift

The biggest risk with an AI making all the art is that every picture comes out in a different style. Judges notice straight away. The fix is to lock the style first and make everything against it.

  1. Step 1 · Style sheet
    Straight after reveal night: Codex makes one key image of the studio in the theme, plus the palette. Luqman approves it. This becomes the style reference for everything else.
  2. Step 2 · Writing
    Name, 7 customers, the last customer. Luqman approves. Claude checks it's all English and short enough for a phone bubble.
  3. Step 3 · 3D models
    Codex writes Blender scripts. Claude runs them, exports GLB, and applies the one toon material, so the models match even if they're simple.
  4. Step 4 · UI + cover
    Made last, against the approved style sheet, so the cover looks like the real game.

How Claude briefs Codex

Each job is sent from the terminal (codex exec) with: the approved style sheet, exact sizes, the file name to save, and "English text only". What comes back goes into the game's folder.

Luqman's approval checkpoints

① style sheet · ② customer writing · ③ models in the game · ④ cover. A "no" at any point means a redo with his notes. There are only four checkpoints, so he's never stuck reviewing all day.

Tech checklist (for when we build)

Godot settings

  • 720×1280, stretch canvas_items, aspect expand, portrait
  • Compatibility renderer
  • Web export: thread support OFF, index.html at the zip root

The pot

  • ~24 widths × ~32 sides = a light mesh, rebuilt every frame
  • Shelf copies are frozen meshes, so they cost almost nothing
  • Autoplay bot checks that 3 stars are reachable on every wish

Phone gotchas

  • Sound only after the first tap ("tap to open the shop")
  • Thumb must not cover the pot, so the touch area sits to the side
  • Build under 50 MB. Big UI, text ~24px+
